BIG BEAR LAKE

BIG BEAR CITY · SAN BERNARDINO MOUNTAINS
Up in the San Bernardino Mountains, where the air’s thin, the pines are tall, and the bass don’t take kindly to city folk noise.
Big Bear at 6,700 feet — cold, clear, permit water.
SPECIES
rainbow troutlargemouth & smallmouth basschannel catfishbluegillcrappie
ACCESS
bank-friendlyboat-goodkayak-good
VIBE
mountainfamilycold mountain watercold watercrowded weekends
BIG BEAR LAKE

ACCESS & FEES

DAY USE
Public mountain water — the MWD public ramps don’t charge a gate fee. The permit is the cost.
LAUNCH
FREE launch at the two public ramps. Every vessel still needs the Lake Use Permit: $46/day motorized · $29/day kayak, canoe or float tube (2026 rates)
ACCESS
Shore fishing from points around the lake, multiple ramps, kayak-friendly if you’re strong-armed. Quagga inspection is mandatory before any vessel touches water.
HOURS
Generally daylight hours; some ramps close at dusk, some stay open later in summer—check posted signs.
CLOSURES
Winter can shut down some access points with snow and ice; chain requirements on the mountain roads are no joke.
PERMIT
California sport fishing license required — and every vessel needs the Lake Use Permit after quagga inspection.
BIG BEAR LAKE

SPECIES & STOCKING

PRIMARY
Rainbow trout, largemouth bass, smallmouth bass
SECONDARY
Crappie, bluegill, catfish (channel)
STOCKING
Rainbow trout stocked regularly through cooler months; schedule varies by season and DFW plans.
SIZE
Trout commonly in the 12–18″ range, with some pushin’ 20″ on a good day; bass in the 2–5 lb range, with the odd bigger one if you know where to knock.
BIG BEAR LAKE

SEASONS & TIMING

BEST MONTHS
April–June, September–November
BEST TIME OF DAY
Early morning and late evening for bass; midday can still produce trout in cooler months.
SEASONAL PATTERNS
SPRING
Bass moving shallow to spawn; trout active in cooling water.
SUMMER
Bass go deeper in the day; trout get picky as water warms.
FALL
Both bass and trout fire up before winter.
WINTER
Trout focus; bass slow down, but can still be coaxed with slow presentations.
BIG BEAR LAKE

TACTICS & GEAR

TOP TECHNIQUES
  • Trout: Still-fishing with nightcrawlers, PowerBait, small jigs; trolling with small spoons or plugs.
  • Bass: Drop shot, Carolina rig, shaky head, topwater early/late, crankbaits along rocky points and timber.
BAITS & LURES
Trout: Nightcrawlers, mealworms, PowerBait, small inline spinners, spoons. · Bass: Soft plastics (worms, creatures), jigs, drop-shot weights, topwater frogs/poppers in low light.
ROD & LINE SETUPS
Trout: 6′–6′6″ light to medium-light spinning rod, 4–8 lb test. · Bass: 6′6″–7′ medium to medium-heavy rod, 8–15 lb test depending on cover.
BIG BEAR LAKE

FACILITIES & SAFETY

RESTROOMS & SHADE
Available at most major ramps and parks; shade is your friend in summer sun.
RAMPS & RENTALS
Multiple ramps around the lake; marinas offer boat rentals, fuel, and basic supplies.
CELL SERVICE
Fair to good around town and main ramps; gets spotty in some coves and back sides.
SAFETY NOTES
  • Cold water year-round—life jacket isn’t optional if you’re in a small boat or kayak.
  • Afternoon winds can kick up; don’t get cocky in a tin can.
  • Winter driving: chains may be required; mountain roads don’t forgive foolishness.

GREEN VALLEY LAKE

GREEN VALLEY LAKE · SAN BERNARDINO MOUNTAINS
Tucked up in the San Bernardino Mountains, not far from Big Bear but with a mood all its own—smaller, quieter, and mean when it wants to be.
PHOTO COMING SOON

ACCESS
bank-friendlyrental boats onlyno motorsno float tubes

GREEN VALLEY LAKE

ACCESS & FEES

DAY USE
Private lake that sells day passes to the public — daily fishing pass at the boathouse: $20 adult · $10 kids under 16 · $15 seniors, military & vets
LAUNCH
No private boats, no motors — rowboats, pedal boats and kayaks rent at the boathouse
ACCESS
Shore fishing around most of the lake; no fishing from the dam, the boat dock area, or the swim beach
HOURS
5:30 AM – 8 PM, Opening Day (mid-May) to Closing Day, weather permitting
CLOSURES
Frozen most winters — this is a mountain season, not a year-round one
PERMIT
California fishing license (16+) required, and the GVL pass has to be visible on you
GREEN VALLEY LAKE

SPECIES & STOCKING

PRIMARY
Rainbow trout — stocked hard, roughly a dozen plants a season at 250–800 lbs apiece
SECONDARY
Bass (catch and release, lures only), crappie, redear sunfish
STOCKING
Jess Ranch and CDFW trout through the season; lake record rainbow is 13 lb 3 oz
SIZE
Trout commonly 12–18″, with some larger; bass mostly 1–4 lbs, with a few bigger ones hiding in the structure.
GREEN VALLEY LAKE

SEASONS & TIMING

BEST MONTHS
April–June, September–November
BEST TIME OF DAY
Early morning and evening for bass; trout can bite all day in cooler seasons.
SEASONAL PATTERNS
SPRING
Bass shallow, trout active.
SUMMER
Trout deeper and pickier; bass hold to shade and structure.
FALL
Both species feed hard before winter.
WINTER
Trout focus; bass slow but not dead.
GREEN VALLEY LAKE

TACTICS & GEAR

TOP TECHNIQUES
  • Trout: Still-fishing with bait, small jigs, trolling light lures.
  • Bass: Catch and release, lures only — no bait on bass here, and they check.
BAITS & LURES
Trout: Nightcrawlers, mealworms, PowerBait, small spinners and spoons. · Bass: Soft plastics (worms, tubes), small jigs, drop-shot rigs.
ROD & LINE SETUPS
Trout: 6′–6′6″ light to medium-light spinning rod, 4–8 lb test. · Bass: 6′6″–7′ medium rod, 8–12 lb test.
GREEN VALLEY LAKE

FACILITIES & SAFETY

RESTROOMS & SHADE
Basic facilities near ramp and shoreline; shade trees in spots.
RAMPS & RENTALS
No private ramp — rentals at the boathouse; the general store in town has bait and tackle.
CELL SERVICE
Fair around the lake, can drop in some coves.
SAFETY NOTES
  • Cold water year-round; life jacket recommended, especially for kayak and small boats.
  • Afternoon winds can chop the water; keep your craft under control.
  • Winter road conditions can be rough; chains may be needed.

SILVERWOOD LAKE

HELENDALE / VICTORVILLE · SAN BERNARDINO HIGH DESERT
Out past Helendale, where the desert meets the hills and the state park runs the show like a well-oiled machine.
Silverwood on a summer day — the swim crowd leaves at dusk, the bass don’t.
SPECIES
largemouth bassstriped bass (seasonal)channel catfishrainbow troutbluegillcrappie
ACCESS
bank-friendlyboat-goodkayak-good
VIBE

SILVERWOOD LAKE

ACCESS & FEES

DAY USE
State park day use: $10 per vehicle — $20 on peak summer weekends and holidays
LAUNCH
$10 per vessel, separate from the vehicle admission
ACCESS
Shore fishing from banks and designated areas, multiple ramps, good for boats and kayaks. No fishing at launch ramps, courtesy docks, or swim areas.
HOURS
Park hours generally daylight to dusk; some areas may have extended hours in summer.
CLOSURES
Park can close or limit access during extreme weather or maintenance; water levels can vary.
PERMIT
California sport fishing license required; special regulations may apply—check CDFW and park postings.
SILVERWOOD LAKE

SPECIES & STOCKING

PRIMARY
Largemouth bass, striped bass (seasonal/variable), catfish (channel)
SECONDARY
Rainbow trout (seasonal), bluegill, crappie
STOCKING
Trout stocked in cooler months; stripers vary by year and management.
SIZE
Bass commonly 2–5 lbs, with trophies over 6–8 lbs possible; stripers can run larger when present; catfish in the 5–15 lb range, with bigger ones out there.
SILVERWOOD LAKE

SEASONS & TIMING

BEST MONTHS
March–June, September–November
BEST TIME OF DAY
Early morning and late evening for bass; night can be good for catfish in summer.
SEASONAL PATTERNS
SPRING
Bass shallow for spawn; trout active.
SUMMER
Bass deeper; catfish more active at night; trout sparse.
FALL
Bass and stripers feed up; water cools, fish get aggressive.
WINTER
Trout and catfish focus; bass slower but still catchable.
SILVERWOOD LAKE

TACTICS & GEAR

TOP TECHNIQUES
  • Bass: Drop shot, Carolina rig, jigs, crankbaits, topwater early/late.
  • Striped bass: Trolling with spoons or plugs, casting swimbaits and jigs.
  • Catfish: Bottom fishing with cut bait, nightcrawlers, stink bait.
  • Trout: Still-fishing with bait, small jigs, trolling light lures in season.
BAITS & LURES
Bass: Soft plastics (worms, creatures), jigs, drop-shot rigs, crankbaits, topwater. · Stripers: Spoons, swimbaits, jigs, trolling plugs. · Catfish: Cut bait, nightcrawlers, prepared catfish baits. · Trout: Nightcrawlers, PowerBait, small spinners/spoons.
ROD & LINE SETUPS
Bass: 6′6″–7′ medium to medium-heavy, 10–17 lb test. · Stripers: 7′ medium-heavy to heavy, 12–20 lb test. · Catfish: 7′ medium-heavy to heavy, 15–25 lb test. · Trout: 6′–6′6″ light to medium-light, 4–8 lb test.
SILVERWOOD LAKE

FACILITIES & SAFETY

RESTROOMS & SHADE
Good facilities around main areas; shade structures and picnic tables available.
RAMPS & RENTALS
Multiple ramps; marina offers boat rentals, fuel, and basic supplies.
CELL SERVICE
Fair to good in most areas; can drop in some coves.
SAFETY NOTES
  • Summer heat is no joke—water, shade, and sunscreen are mandatory, not optional.
  • Afternoon winds can whip the lake up; respect the chop in small boats.
  • Watch for submerged structure and changing water levels.
SILVERWOOD LAKE

LAKE ELSINORE · RIVERSIDE / INLAND EMPIRE
Down in the Elsinore Valley, where the heat shimmers and the bass act like they’ve got a union and a lawyer.
Elsinore from the hills — 3,000 acres of natural water. Diamond Valley sits one valley over.

LAKE ELSINORE / DIAMOND VALLEY

ACCESS & FEES

DAY USE
Elsinore: public beaches and long free shoreline stretches — no gate fee. Diamond Valley: $11 parking per vehicle
LAUNCH
Elsinore: Launch Pointe — $10 launch, parking included, plus a $10 Lake Use Fee per vessel. Diamond Valley: launch through the marina — 951-926-7201 for current fees
ACCESS
Elsinore: 14 miles of shoreline, public beaches, a 4-mile levee. Diamond Valley: designated shore fishing and rentals through Diamond Valley Marina
HOURS
Elsinore: 6 AM to half an hour before sunset. Diamond Valley: marina gate hours — check before you drive
CLOSURES
Elsinore: algae blooms and wind advisories can change the day — check the city lake report. Diamond Valley: no dogs (wildlife refuge), quagga zero tolerance
PERMIT
California fishing license required on both — Diamond Valley is public water, no private-lake exemption
LAKE ELSINORE / DIAMOND VALLEY

PRIMARY
Elsinore: largemouth bass, channel catfish · Diamond Valley: largemouth bass (trophy-oriented)
SECONDARY
Elsinore: bluegill, crappie, occasional trout in cooler months · Diamond Valley: bluegill, catfish (varies by stocking)
STOCKING
Elsinore: occasional trout stockings; bass and cats self-sustaining. Diamond Valley: stocked since fill-up — Florida largemouth, smallmouth, trout and catfish. A managed fishery, not a private one.
SIZE
Elsinore: bass 2–5 lbs, trophies over 6–8 lbs; cats 5–15 lbs. Diamond Valley: bass 3–8 lbs, trophies over 10 lbs; well-managed fishery.
